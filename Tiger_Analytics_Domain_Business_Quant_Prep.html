3. Domain Knowledge — BFSI & Insurance

This is YOUR strongest domain. Tiger clients include banks, insurers, and financial services firms. Your Manulife experience gives you an edge — leverage it.

3.1 Key Concepts You Must Know Cold

Q1 — What is Credit Risk and how is it measured? Domain

Credit risk is the risk that a borrower fails to repay a loan or meet contractual obligations.

Key metrics:
  • PD (Probability of Default): Likelihood a borrower defaults within a time horizon (typically 12 months or lifetime)
  • LGD (Loss Given Default): Percentage of exposure lost if default occurs (1 − recovery rate)
  • EAD (Exposure at Default): Total amount exposed at time of default
  • ECL (Expected Credit Loss): ECL = PD × LGD × EAD — the provisioning amount under IFRS 9
Your anchor: "At Manulife, I built a dual-output XGBoost model estimating PD and LGD simultaneously, computing ECL at the borrower level for IFRS 9-aligned risk reporting."

Q2 — Explain IFRS 9 staging for impairment Domain

IFRS 9 requires classifying financial assets into three stages:
StageConditionECL Calculation
Stage 1No significant increase in credit risk since origination12-month ECL
Stage 2Significant increase in credit risk (e.g., 30+ DPD)Lifetime ECL
Stage 3Credit-impaired / defaulted (90+ DPD)Lifetime ECL (interest on net)
Why it matters: Models must distinguish between stages to calculate correct provisions. Migration between stages directly impacts P&L.

Q3 — What is Basel III and its relevance to data science? Domain

Basel III is a global regulatory framework for bank capital adequacy, stress testing, and market liquidity risk.

Data Science connections:
  • Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) approach: Banks build internal PD/LGD/EAD models — exactly what you built
  • Stress testing: Scenario-based models predicting portfolio losses under adverse economic conditions
  • Capital adequacy: Risk-weighted assets determine minimum capital requirements — driven by credit risk models
  • Model validation: Requires rigorous backtesting (discrimination: AUC/KS, calibration: Hosmer-Lemeshow)

Q4 — What are key fraud detection patterns in insurance? Domain

Common fraud patterns:
  • Staged accidents: Unusually high claim frequency from same claimant or provider network
  • Inflated claims: Claim amount significantly above median for similar claim types
  • Identity fraud: Mismatched personal details, short policy tenure before claim
  • Provider collusion: Network analysis reveals clusters of providers with abnormally high claim rates
Your anchor: "My fraud detection model identified FICO score, DTI ratio, and loan purpose as top indicators — similar network/behavioral signals apply in insurance claims."

Q5 — Explain insurance pricing / actuarial concepts Domain

Key terms:
  • Pure Premium: Expected claim cost per policy (frequency × severity)
  • Gross Premium: Pure premium + expense loading + profit margin
  • Loss Ratio: Claims paid / premiums earned (target: 60–70%)
  • Combined Ratio: Loss ratio + expense ratio (< 100% = underwriting profit)
  • Adverse Selection: High-risk individuals more likely to buy insurance
  • Moral Hazard: Insured parties take more risk because they're covered
Your anchor: "My XGBoost premium prediction model used age, medical history, lifestyle, and demographics — with SHAP-based explainability so underwriters could understand pricing drivers."

Q6 — What is AML / KYC and how does data science help? Domain

  • AML (Anti-Money Laundering): ML models detect suspicious transaction patterns (structuring, layering, round-tripping)
  • KYC (Know Your Customer): Identity verification, risk scoring of customers at onboarding
  • Transaction monitoring: Rule-based + ML-based systems flag anomalies in real-time
  • SAR (Suspicious Activity Report): Automated flagging reduces manual review burden

4. Domain Knowledge — CPG, Retail & Pharma

Tiger Analytics Toronto focuses heavily on CPG & Retail clients. Even though your core is BFSI, you must speak this language fluently.

4.1 CPG / FMCG Domain

Q7 — What is Trade Promotion Optimization (TPO)? Domain

Trade promotions (discounts, BOGO, displays) consume 15–20% of CPG revenue but ~60% fail to break even.

TPO uses analytics to:
  • Predict incremental lift from each promotion type
  • Optimize promotion timing, depth, and vehicle (price cut vs. display vs. feature ad)
  • Separate baseline sales from incremental sales (decomposition)
  • Measure cannibalization (promotion steals from non-promoted SKUs) and halo effect (boosts related products)
Your anchor: "At WNS, I analyzed IMS/Nielsen data to identify high-growth product segments — similar decomposition logic applies to trade promotion lift measurement."

Q8 — What is Price Elasticity and how is it used? Domain

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) = % Change in Quantity / % Change in Price

  • Elastic (|PED| > 1): Small price change → big demand change (luxury goods, competitive products)
  • Inelastic (|PED| < 1): Demand barely changes with price (necessities, brand-loyal products)
  • Cross-price elasticity: How competitor pricing affects your demand
Application: CPG companies use price elasticity models to set optimal price points that maximize revenue or margin, not just volume.

Q9 — Explain Demand Forecasting in CPG Domain

  • Baseline forecast: Historical demand adjusted for trend and seasonality
  • Promotional uplift: Incremental demand from planned promotions
  • Causal factors: Weather, holidays, competitor actions, economic indicators
  • Granularity: SKU × Store × Week level for execution
  • Methods: ARIMA, Prophet, Gradient Boosting, or hybrid approaches
  • Key metric: WMAPE (Weighted Mean Absolute Percentage Error) — standard in CPG
Your anchor: "My Prophet-based forecasting at Manulife operated at cost-center × daily granularity — the same decomposition approach (trend + seasonality + holidays) directly transfers to CPG demand forecasting at SKU × week level."

4.2 Retail Domain

Q10 — What is RFM Analysis? Domain

RFM = Recency, Frequency, Monetary — a customer segmentation technique:
  • Recency: How recently did the customer purchase? (lower = better)
  • Frequency: How often do they purchase? (higher = better)
  • Monetary: How much do they spend? (higher = better)
Segment customers into tiers (Champions, Loyal, At Risk, Lost) to tailor marketing strategies. Each customer gets scored 1–5 on each dimension.

Q11 — What is Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)? Domain

CLV = Average Order Value × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan

More sophisticated: CLV = Σ (Margin_t / (1+d)^t) — discounted future margins over predicted lifetime.

Why it matters: CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) must be < CLV for a sustainable business. CLV/CAC ratio of 3:1 is a common benchmark.

5. Domain Knowledge — Cloud & FinOps

Q13 — What is FinOps and why does it matter? Domain

FinOps (Cloud Financial Operations) is a cultural practice that brings financial accountability to cloud spending.
  • Inform: Visibility into who's spending what (showback/chargeback)
  • Optimize: Right-sizing, reserved instances, spot instances, anomaly detection
  • Operate: Continuous governance, budgets, alerts, automation
Your anchor: "My entire Manulife flagship project IS FinOps — I built the Inform layer (Grafana dashboards), the Optimize layer (Prophet anomaly detection), and the Operate layer (automated alerts via Azure Functions)."

Q14 — What is anomaly detection and how did you approach it? Domain

Anomaly detection identifies data points that deviate significantly from expected patterns.

Approaches:
  • Statistical: Z-score, IQR, Grubbs test — for univariate, static thresholds
  • Time-series: Prophet forecast bands, ARIMA residuals, exponential smoothing — for temporal patterns
  • ML-based: Isolation Forest, Autoencoders, DBSCAN — for multivariate, complex patterns
Your approach: Prophet with 95% confidence interval (yhat_upper), 2-consecutive-day rule to reduce false positives, per-cost-center granularity.

6. Business Problem Solving — Frameworks

Tiger Analytics is a consulting firm. They expect you to approach problems like a consultant: clarify → structure → hypothesize → analyze → recommend. NEVER jump to a solution.

6.1 The MECE Framework

MECE = Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive

Every breakdown of a problem must be:
  • Mutually Exclusive: No overlap between categories
  • Collectively Exhaustive: All possibilities covered
Example: Revenue decline analysis → MECE split:
  • Revenue = Price × Volume
  • Volume = New Customers × Orders/Customer × Items/Order
  • Or: Revenue = Online + Offline → (by channel, by region, by product)

6.2 Issue Tree Framework

Break any problem into sub-problems recursively: 
Profit Declining?
├── Revenue declining?
│   ├── Price decreased?
│   │   ├── Competitive pressure?
│   │   └── Promotional overspend?
│   └── Volume decreased?
│       ├── Fewer customers?
│       ├── Lower frequency?
│       └── Smaller basket size?
└── Costs increasing?
    ├── COGS rising? (input costs, supplier issues)
    ├── Opex rising? (headcount, rent, marketing)
    └── One-time charges?

6.3 Market Sizing / Guesstimate Framework

Top-Down approach:
  1. Start with a large known number (population, total market)
  2. Apply progressive filters to narrow down
  3. Multiply by a per-unit value
Bottom-Up approach:
  1. Start from a single unit (one store, one customer)
  2. Estimate unit economics
  3. Scale up to total market
Always: State assumptions, round numbers, sanity-check the final answer.

6.4 The Analytics Problem Solving Framework (CRISP-DM)

  1. Business Understanding: What decision will this inform? What's the KPI?
  2. Data Understanding: What data exists? Quality? Granularity? Freshness?
  3. Data Preparation: Cleaning, feature engineering, handling missing values
  4. Modeling: Start simple (logistic regression) → graduate to complex (XGBoost). Cross-validate.
  5. Evaluation: Business-aligned metrics, not just accuracy. ROI quantification.
  6. Deployment: How will stakeholders use it? Monitoring? Retraining cadence?

6.5 Hypothesis-Driven Approach

Instead of "let me explore the data," lead with:
  1. State a hypothesis: "I believe churn is driven by poor onboarding experience in the first 30 days."
  2. Define what data would confirm/refute it: "I'd look at feature usage in weeks 1–4 vs. churn at day 90."
  3. Analyze and revise: Present findings and update hypothesis.
Why Tiger loves this: Consulting clients pay for speed and direction. Exploratory analysis without hypotheses wastes budget.

7. Case Studies & Guesstimates (20 Q&A)

7.1 Case Studies

Q15 — A CPG company's market share dropped 3% in 6 months. Diagnose. CaseMedium

Step 1 — Clarify: Which product/category? Which market/region? What data is available?

Step 2 — Structure (MECE):
  • Internal factors:
    • Product issues: quality, formulation change, packaging
    • Pricing: recent price increase vs. competitor holds
    • Distribution: lost shelf space, fewer stores carrying product
    • Promotion: reduced trade spend or ineffective campaigns
  • External factors:
    • Competitive entry: new competitor or aggressive pricing from existing
    • Category dynamics: is the entire category shrinking?
    • Consumer trends: shift to premium/value/organic/private label
Step 3 — Prioritize: Check distribution (ACV) and competitive pricing first — these explain most CPG share shifts.
Step 4 — Recommend: If distribution loss → retailer negotiation + trade incentives. If pricing → elasticity analysis to find optimal price point.

Q16 — An e-commerce company sees conversion rate drop from 3.2% to 2.1%. What do you investigate? CaseMedium

Decompose the funnel:
  1. Traffic source mix change? (Organic vs. paid — paid often has lower intent)
  2. Device mix? (Mobile conversion is typically 50% lower than desktop)
  3. Landing page performance? (Bounce rate change?)
  4. Product page → Cart (add-to-cart rate dropped?)
  5. Cart → Checkout (cart abandonment increased?)
  6. Checkout → Purchase (payment failures? UX change? new friction?)
Data I'd request: Conversion by device, by traffic source, by product category, over time (was it sudden or gradual?)
Sudden drop = technical issue (checkout bug, page load time)
Gradual decline = market/competitive/pricing issue

Q17 — A bank wants to reduce loan default rates. Design an analytics solution. CaseMedium

  1. Business goal: Reduce defaults by X% while maintaining loan approval volume
  2. Data: Borrower demographics, credit bureau data, income, employment, loan characteristics, payment history
  3. Feature engineering: DTI ratio, payment-to-income ratio, bureau utilization, months-since-last-delinquency
  4. Model: XGBoost for PD prediction (your proven approach), calibrated probabilities
  5. Deployment: Risk scorecard integrated into loan origination system, decision threshold based on bank's risk appetite
  6. Monitoring: PSI (Population Stability Index) for input drift, KS/AUC tracking monthly
  7. Business impact: Every 1% reduction in default rate ≈ $X million in avoided losses (quantify!)
Your anchor: Reference your credit risk project directly.

Q18 — A pharma company wants to predict which patients will stop taking their medication. How would you approach this? CaseMedium

  1. Define non-adherence: No refill within X days of expected refill date (e.g., MPR < 80%)
  2. Data sources: Claims/pharmacy data, demographics, comorbidities, drug complexity, prior adherence history
  3. Key features: Number of concurrent medications, out-of-pocket cost, days since last refill, prescriber specialty, first-time vs. chronic patient
  4. Model: Survival analysis (time-to-discontinuation) or classification (adherent vs. non-adherent at 6 months)
  5. Intervention: Score patients at fill time → high-risk patients get proactive outreach (reminder calls, nurse support, simplified dosing)
  6. Metric: Proportion of days covered (PDC), impact on health outcomes

Q19 — How would you measure the ROI of a loyalty program for a retail chain? CaseMedium

ROI = (Incremental Revenue − Program Cost) / Program Cost

Measuring incrementality:
  • Compare loyalty members vs. matched control group (propensity score matching)
  • Measure: purchase frequency lift, basket size lift, retention rate improvement
  • Account for self-selection bias — loyal customers would have spent anyway
  • Use difference-in-differences or instrumental variables for causal estimates
Costs: Reward payouts + technology + marketing + staff training
Hidden benefits: First-party data collection, personalization capability, competitive switching cost

Q20 — A company's customer churn rate spiked from 8% to 14% this quarter. Walk me through your analysis. CaseMedium

  1. Clarify churn definition: Voluntary or involuntary? Subscription cancel or inactivity?
  2. Segment the spike: By tenure (new vs. existing), by product, by acquisition channel, by geography
  3. Timeline: Was the spike sudden (event-driven) or gradual (trend)?
  4. Hypotheses:
    • Pricing change? (check if churn correlates with price increase date)
    • Product/service degradation? (check support tickets, NPS scores)
    • Competitive move? (new entrant, competitor promotion)
    • Seasonal? (compare to same quarter prior year)
    • Cohort-specific? (batch of poorly qualified customers from a campaign)
  5. Analysis: Survival curves by cohort, feature importance from churn prediction model
  6. Recommendation: Targeted retention for high-value at-risk customers, root-cause fix for the driver

Q24 — A retailer is considering adding self-checkout kiosks. Should they? Frame the analysis. CaseHard

Framework — Cost-Benefit Analysis:

Benefits:
  • Labor savings: Each kiosk replaces 0.5–0.7 FTE cashier
  • Throughput: Faster checkout → shorter lines → higher customer satisfaction
  • Peak handling: Can flex capacity without hiring seasonal staff
Costs:
  • Capital: $25K–$50K per kiosk + installation
  • Maintenance: ~$3K–5K/year per kiosk
  • Shrinkage increase: Self-checkout has 2–4× higher theft rate
  • Customer experience risk: Some demographics prefer human interaction
Analysis approach:
  • Pilot in 5–10 stores, measure: checkout time, shrinkage delta, customer satisfaction (NPS), labor hours saved
  • Calculate payback period per kiosk
  • A/B test: stores with vs. without self-checkout
Recommendation framework: Proceed if payback < 18 months AND shrinkage increase < labor savings.

Q25 — How would you use data science to improve hospital bed utilization? CaseMedium

  1. Predict patient length of stay (LOS): Features: diagnosis, age, comorbidities, admission source. Model: Gradient Boosting regression.

Q26 — A food delivery company wants to reduce delivery time. How would you approach this? CaseMedium

8. Quantitative Aptitude — Number Systems & Arithmetic

Speed is key. Tiger's aptitude section is timed. Practice mental math — no calculator allowed.

Q27 — Percentage shortcuts Quant

  • x% of y = y% of x (e.g., 8% of 50 = 50% of 8 = 4)
  • Successive % change: a% then b% → Net = a + b + (ab/100). E.g., 20% increase then 20% decrease = 20 + (−20) + (20×(−20)/100) = −4% (NOT zero!)
  • Quick 15%: 10% + 5% (half of 10%)
  • Percentage change: (New − Old) / Old × 100
  • If A is 25% more than B, then B is 20% less than A (not 25% less!)

Q28 — Profit & Loss Quant

  • Profit% = (SP − CP) / CP × 100
  • If CP = 80, SP = 100: Profit% = 25%, NOT 20%
  • Discount: SP = MP × (1 − Discount%)
  • Two successive discounts of 20% and 10%: Effective = 1 − (0.8 × 0.9) = 1 − 0.72 = 28% (NOT 30%)
Practice Problem: An article marked at $500 is sold at two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. Find the selling price.
Answer: 500 × 0.8 × 0.9 = $360

Q29 — Ratio & Proportion Quant

  • If A:B = 3:4 and B:C = 5:6 → A:B:C = 15:20:24 (make B common)
  • Mixture problems: Use alligation rule: (Cheaper − Mean) / (Mean − Dearer) = Quantity of Dearer / Quantity of Cheaper
Practice: Mix milk at $40/L and water at $0/L to get mixture at $32/L. What's the ratio?
Answer: (0 − 32)/(32 − 40) = 32/8 = 4:1 → Milk:Water = 4:1

Q30 — Time, Speed & Distance Quant

  • Average speed for equal distances: 2ab/(a+b), NOT (a+b)/2
  • Example: 60 km at 30 km/h and 60 km at 60 km/h → Avg speed = 2×30×60/(30+60) = 3600/90 = 40 km/h
  • Relative speed: Same direction = |a−b|, Opposite direction = a+b
  • Trains crossing: Total distance = sum of lengths, speed = relative speed

Q31 — Work & Time Quant

  • If A does work in 10 days, B in 15 days: Combined rate = 1/10 + 1/15 = 5/30 = 1/6 → Together: 6 days
  • If A and B together take 6 days, A alone takes 10 days: B's rate = 1/6 − 1/10 = 4/60 = 1/15 → B alone: 15 days
Practice: A does a job in 12 days, B in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. How many more days for B to finish?
Answer: Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36. In 4 days: 20/36 = 5/9 done. Remaining: 4/9. B alone: (4/9) / (1/18) = 8 days.

Q32 — Simple & Compound Interest Quant

  • SI = PRT/100
  • CI = P(1 + R/100)^T − P
  • Difference between CI and SI for 2 years = P(R/100)²
Practice: $10,000 at 10% for 2 years. Find SI and CI.
SI = 10000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = $2,000
CI = 10000(1.1)² − 10000 = 12100 − 10000 = $2,100
Difference = $100 = 10000 × (0.1)² ✓

9. Quantitative Aptitude — Algebra & Equations

Q33 — Number Series Patterns Quant

Common patterns to look for:
  • Arithmetic: Constant difference (2, 5, 8, 11... → +3)
  • Geometric: Constant ratio (3, 6, 12, 24... → ×2)
  • Fibonacci: Sum of two preceding (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8...)
  • Differences of differences: 2, 5, 10, 17, 26... → differences: 3, 5, 7, 9 → second difference: 2, 2, 2
  • Alternating operations: +2, ×3, +2, ×3...
  • Prime numbers: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13...
  • Squares ± constant: 1, 4, 9, 16... (n²) or 2, 5, 10, 17 (n²+1)
Practice: Find next: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
Differences: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 → Answer: 42

Q34 — Age Problems Quant

Template: Set variables, write equations from conditions.

Practice: Father is 3 times as old as son. 12 years later, father will be twice as old. Find ages.
Let son = x. Father = 3x.
3x + 12 = 2(x + 12)
3x + 12 = 2x + 24
x = 12. Son = 12, Father = 36.

Q35 — Averages & Weighted Averages Quant

  • Weighted Average = Σ(weight × value) / Σ(weights)
  • If one item is removed: New avg = (Old sum − removed item) / (n−1)
Practice: Average of 5 numbers is 20. If 30 is added, new average?
Sum = 100. New sum = 130. New avg = 130/6 = 21.67

11. Quantitative Aptitude — Probability & Combinatorics

Tiger tests probability HEAVILY. You already covered Bayes' theorem in the technical round. This section covers additional patterns they ask in the aptitude round.

Q38 — Permutations & Combinations Quick Reference Quant

  • nPr = n! / (n−r)! — order matters (arrangements)
  • nCr = n! / [r!(n−r)!] — order doesn't matter (selections)
  • Key identity: nCr = nC(n−r)
  • With repetition: nⁿ arrangements (e.g., 4-digit PIN from 0–9 = 10⁴ = 10,000)
Practice: How many ways to select a committee of 3 from 8 people?
8C3 = 8! / (3! × 5!) = (8×7×6)/(3×2×1) = 56

Q39 — Probability with Cards QuantMedium

Standard deck: 52 cards, 4 suits (13 each), 12 face cards, 4 aces.

Practice: Probability of drawing 2 aces from a deck without replacement?
P = (4/52) × (3/51) = 12/2652 = 1/221 ≈ 0.0045

Practice: P(at least one ace in 2 draws)?
P = 1 − P(no ace) = 1 − (48/52 × 47/51) = 1 − 2256/2652 = 396/2652 = 33/221 ≈ 0.149

Q40 — Probability with Dice Quant

Two dice — total outcomes: 36
  • P(sum = 7) = 6/36 = 1/6 (most likely sum)
  • P(sum = 2 or 12) = 1/36 each
  • P(at least one 6) = 1 − P(no 6) = 1 − (5/6)² = 1 − 25/36 = 11/36
  • P(doubles) = 6/36 = 1/6

Q41 — Balls in Bags QuantMedium

Problem: A bag has 5 red, 4 blue, 3 green balls. Two balls are drawn without replacement. What's P(both red)?
Total = 12. P = (5/12) × (4/11) = 20/132 = 5/33 ≈ 0.152

Problem: P(one red and one blue)?
P = (5/12)(4/11) + (4/12)(5/11) = 40/132 = 10/33 ≈ 0.303 (order can be either way)

Q42 — Expected Value Problems QuantMedium

E[X] = Σ x × P(x)

Problem: A game costs $10 to play. You flip a coin: heads → win $25, tails → win nothing. Should you play?
E[profit] = 0.5 × ($25 − $10) + 0.5 × ($0 − $10) = 0.5 × 15 + 0.5 × (−10) = 7.5 − 5 = $2.50 expected profit. Yes, play.

Problem: Insurance company: 0.01 probability of $100,000 claim, annual premium $1,500. Expected profit per policy?
E[profit] = $1,500 − 0.01 × $100,000 = $1,500 − $1,000 = $500

Q43 — DI Speed Tips Quant

Types you'll encounter: Bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, tables, combination charts.

Speed strategies:
  • Read the question first, then the chart — don't study the entire chart upfront
  • Approximate aggressively: 398 → 400, 7.3% → 7%. Exact answers rarely needed.
  • Percentage of total from pie chart: Don't calculate the angle — estimate the proportion visually

13. Quantitative Aptitude — Logical Reasoning & Puzzles

Q45 — Syllogisms Quant

Rules:
  • All A are B + All B are C → All A are C ✓
  • All A are B + Some B are C → Some A are C ✗ (not necessarily)
  • No A are B + All B are C → No A are C ✓
Practice: "All managers are leaders. Some leaders are innovators."
Conclusion: "Some managers are innovators" — NOT necessarily true. The overlap could be between non-manager leaders and innovators.

Q46 — Seating Arrangement QuantMedium

Problem: 6 people (A–F) sit in a circle. A is opposite D. B is to the left of A. C is not adjacent to A. Where does everyone sit?

Approach: Fix A's position. Place D opposite. Place B to A's left. Try positions for C, E, F satisfying constraints.
Key tip: In circular arrangements, fix one person to avoid counting rotations. Possible arrangements of n people in a circle = (n−1)!

Q47 — Blood Relations Quant

Problem: "A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. E is D's mother." What is A's relation to D?

Draw the tree: E → D → C → B, and A is B's sister (so A is also C's child)
A is D's granddaughter.

Q49 — Classic Puzzles Tiger Asks QuantHard

Puzzle 1 — Light Switches: 3 switches outside a room, 3 bulbs inside. You can enter the room only once. How do you identify which switch controls which bulb?
Solution: Turn switch 1 ON for 10 minutes. Turn it OFF, turn switch 2 ON. Enter room. Hot bulb = switch 1. Lit bulb = switch 2. Cold & off = switch 3.

Puzzle 2 — River Crossing: A farmer has a fox, chicken, and grain. Boat fits farmer + 1 item. Fox eats chicken, chicken eats grain if left alone.
Solution: Take chicken across → return → take fox across → bring chicken back → take grain across → return → take chicken across.

Puzzle 3 — 25 Horses: 25 horses, 5 lanes. Find top 3 in minimum races (no timer).
Solution: 5 group races + 1 race of winners + 1 more = 7 races.
Race 5 groups of 5. Race the 5 winners (eliminates 3 groups). From remaining, race: 2nd & 3rd of winner's group + 2nd of runner-up's group = 6th race identifies top 3.

Q50 — Direction Sense Quant

Problem: A starts facing North. Turns right, walks 5 km. Turns right again, walks 3 km. Turns left, walks 2 km. Which direction is A facing?

North → Right = East → Right = South → Left = East
Tip: Draw it on paper. Track direction at each turn.

14. Resume Deep-Dive — Domain Q&A for Your Projects

In this round, they'll probe your projects for domain understanding, not just technical methodology. Be ready to explain the business context and industry implications of every project.

Q51 — "Your anomaly detection saved $12.8M. Walk me through how the business actually saved that money." Resume

The savings came from three mechanisms:

1. Right-sizing: When the model flagged a cost center spending $15K/day against a $9K forecast, the alert email included a resource-level breakdown. The cost center owner could see exactly which VMs or storage accounts were overprovisioned and scale them down. This was the largest savings driver.

2. Zombie resource cleanup: Alerts surfaced resources that were provisioned for projects that had ended — dev/test environments left running, unattached disks, orphaned IP addresses. Without the alerts, these would run indefinitely.

3. Reserved Instance optimization: The anomaly patterns revealed consistent baseline spend for certain services, which justified switching from pay-as-you-go to 1-year reserved instances at 30–40% discount.

The $12.8M figure is the annualized sum across all cost centers — validated by the FinOps team comparing actual spend trajectories before and after model deployment.

Q52 — "Why did you choose XGBoost for credit risk instead of logistic regression, which is the industry standard?" Resume

Good question — and in production at a bank, I would likely use logistic regression for regulatory reasons. Basel/IFRS 9 models require interpretability, audit trails, and monotonic constraints. Logistic regression gives you coefficients that regulators can inspect.

For this project, which was a portfolio risk assessment tool (not a regulatory submission model), XGBoost gave us:
• Better discrimination (higher AUC/KS) on non-linear interactions like income × DTI × tenure
• Built-in feature importance ranking
• We added SHAP for post-hoc explainability, which satisfied the risk officers

If deploying this at a regulated institution, I'd build both: XGBoost as a challenger model to identify patterns, then translate key findings into a scorecard-style logistic regression for production.

Q53 — "Your fraud detection model had F1 > 0.75. Is that good enough for production?" Resume

It depends on the business context — specifically, the cost ratio of false positives vs. false negatives.

In fraud detection:
• False negative (missed fraud): Direct financial loss + regulatory penalty → very expensive
• False positive (flagging legitimate application): Manual review cost + customer friction → moderate cost

F1 > 0.75 is a strong starting point for a severely imbalanced dataset (<2% fraud). In production, I'd:
1. Tune the decision threshold based on the cost matrix (lower threshold to catch more fraud at the expense of more reviews)
2. Move from a binary decision to a tiered risk score: auto-approve (low risk), manual review (medium), auto-reject (high)
3. Track precision@recall curves over time to detect model degradation

The real KPI isn't F1 — it's fraud loss reduction in dollars vs. review cost increase.

Q54 — "You worked on FMCG analytics at WNS. How is analyzing IMS/Nielsen data different from internal sales data?" Resume

Syndicated data (Nielsen/IMS) vs. Internal data serve fundamentally different purposes:

Syndicated (Nielsen/IMS/IRI):
  • Provides market-level view — you see your competitors' performance, not just your own
  • Standardized metrics: market share, distribution (ACV%), price per unit, volume
  • Covers all outlets (sampled) — gives you the full category picture
  • Drawback: Expensive, lagged (typically 4–6 week delay), aggregated (not customer-level)
Internal sales data:
  • Granular: transaction-level, customer-level, real-time
  • No competitive view — you only see your own performance
  • Better for operational decisions (inventory, pricing, promotions)
Best practice: Combine both — internal data for execution, syndicated for strategic positioning and competitive benchmarking.

Q55 — "How would you explain your Prophet model to a non-technical CFO?" Resume

"Think of it like a weather forecast for your Azure bill. The model learns your normal spending patterns — weekday vs. weekend rhythms, month-end spikes, holiday dips — and predicts what tomorrow's spend should look like. When actual spend is significantly above the forecast range for two consecutive days, we flag it as unusual and send your team an alert with exactly which resources are causing the spike. You don't need to understand the math — you just need to know that if you get an alert, something unexpected is happening and it's worth a 5-minute check."

Q56 — "Tell me about a time you had to translate a business problem into a data science solution." Resume

(S) At GMR Delhi Airport, the CCO wanted to increase non-aeronautical revenue from retail and F&B concessions but didn't have visibility into which promotions worked.

(T) My job was to design data-driven campaigns that would demonstrably lift revenue.

(A) I consolidated footfall data, POS transaction data, and promotion calendars across all terminals onto Qlik Sense and Power BI. I segmented passengers by terminal, flight type (domestic/international), and time-of-day. Then I identified high-footfall but low-conversion zones and tested targeted promotions (flash sales timed to departure waves, bundle offers at underperforming outlets).

(R) Generated INR 2 Million in incremental revenue. The key wasn't sophisticated modeling — it was connecting the right data to the right decision at the right time.
