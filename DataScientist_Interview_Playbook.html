4 Project deep-dives — STAR narratives

Have 4 classical ML and 3 GenAI stories ready. Open each with a one-line hook, then STAR.

🏦 Project 1 — Cloud Cost Anomaly Detection (flagship)

Hook: "This is the one that delivered $12.8M CAD in savings, so let me start here."

Situation
Manulife's Azure spend was growing double-digits YoY; cost-centre owners were reacting to monthly invoices instead of daily signals.
Task
Detect anomalous daily spend at the cost-centre level early enough for owners to act, without drowning them in false alerts.
Action
Built a daily time-series pipeline in Azure Data Factory pulling consumption data into PostgreSQL. Trained Facebook Prophet per cost-centre with holiday/weekend seasonality. Flagged deviations above a tuned confidence-interval threshold. Automated resource-level breakdown emails via Azure Functions and surfaced trends in Grafana & Power BI.
Result
$12.8M CAD annualised savings, alert precision tuned to keep noise low, and adoption by finance + FinOps as the source of truth.
Likely follow-up: "Why Prophet and not ARIMA/LSTM?"
Prophet handles multiple seasonalities, holidays and missing days out of the box, is interpretable to non-ML stakeholders, and scales well per-series. ARIMA needed per-series stationarity work; LSTM was overkill given data volume per cost-centre and would have hurt interpretability which was a hard requirement.
"How did you avoid alert fatigue?"
Tuned the CI width per cost-centre based on historical volatility, added a minimum absolute-dollar threshold so tiny percentage swings on small centres didn't alert, and required 2 consecutive breach days for lower-priority centres.
"How did you attribute $12.8M?"
Compared post-intervention run-rate versus Prophet's counterfactual forecast for the same period, cross-checked against FinOps ticket resolutions tagged to alerts. Finance signed off on the methodology.

💳 Project 2 — Credit Risk PD / LGD / ECL

Hook: "IFRS 9-aligned expected credit loss, deployed as a Streamlit tool risk officers actually use."

Situation
Risk team needed on-demand borrower-level ECL, not batch quarterly runs.
Task
Estimate PD and LGD from the same feature set and combine into ECL = PD × LGD × EAD.
Action
Dual XGBoost heads on engineered credit/income/repayment features. Validated with AUC and KS for PD, and calibration curves for LGD. Deployed as Streamlit with SHAP for explainability.
Result
On-demand scoring for risk officers, aligned with IFRS 9 / Basel-style reporting, and interpretable drivers for audit.
"Why XGBoost over logistic regression for PD?"
Logistic gave us the baseline and interpretability floor; XGBoost captured non-linear interactions (DTI × loan purpose × FICO band) and lifted AUC meaningfully. I kept logistic as a challenger and used SHAP to preserve interpretability.
"How do you calibrate PD probabilities?"
Isotonic regression on a held-out set, then reliability diagrams before sign-off. For LGD (bounded [0,1]) I used beta-calibration.

🕵️ Project 3 — Fraudulent Loan Application Detection

Hook: "Classic class-imbalance problem — fraud was under 3% of applications."

Situation
Manual fraud review was slow and inconsistent.
Task
Auto-flag high-risk applications with recall high enough to catch fraud but precision high enough not to swamp reviewers.
Action
SMOTE oversampling on the training fold only (never on validation), Random Forest tuned with GridSearchCV, threshold selected on the precision-recall curve, not accuracy.
Result
F1 > 0.75; top drivers were FICO, DTI, loan purpose. Proposed a RF + GBM ensemble and a real-time inference architecture.
"Why not SMOTE on validation?"
Because validation must reflect production distribution. Synthetic minority samples in validation give optimistic recall and hide model overfitting to the SMOTE manifold.

6 Technical Q&A drills

1. Explain bias–variance trade-off with a project example.
Bias = error from wrong assumptions (underfitting), variance = sensitivity to training data (overfitting). In the credit-risk PD model, a shallow tree had high bias (missed FICO×purpose interactions); a deep unregularised XGBoost overfit. I tuned max_depth, min_child_weight, and used early stopping on a validation fold to land on the sweet spot.
2. Why SMOTE, and when is it wrong?
SMOTE synthesises minority-class points along k-NN lines. Wrong when: (a) minority class is genuinely noisy — you amplify noise; (b) high-dimensional sparse features — synthetic points fall in unrealistic regions; (c) applied on validation/test — leaks optimism. Alternatives: class-weighting, focal loss, threshold tuning.
3. How do you choose the classification threshold?
Never default to 0.5. Pick from the precision-recall curve based on business cost: for fraud, false negatives are more expensive, so I move toward higher recall; for premium pricing, I optimise on business KPIs (loss ratio) not just F1.
4. Prophet vs ARIMA vs LSTM for daily cost series?
Prophet wins when you need interpretability, multiple seasonalities and holidays, and you have many parallel series. ARIMA when the series is stationary and short. LSTM only when you have long history, lots of series, and non-linear autoregressive structure — and you're willing to lose interpretability.
5. Explain SHAP in one minute.
SHAP assigns each feature a contribution to a single prediction based on Shapley values from cooperative game theory — average marginal contribution across all feature-subset orderings. It's locally faithful, globally aggregatable, and model-agnostic (TreeSHAP is exact and fast for XGBoost).
6. Model drift — how do you detect it in production?
Monitor (a) input drift: PSI / KS on features, (b) prediction drift: distribution of scores, (c) performance drift when labels arrive. In the cost anomaly model I also watched alert precision weekly. Trigger retraining on threshold breach, not on a fixed cadence.
7. Write a SQL query for top-3 spending resources per cost-centre. 
SELECT cost_centre, resource_id, total_cost
FROM (
  SELECT cost_centre, resource_id,
         SUM(cost) AS total_cost,
         ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
           PARTITION BY cost_centre
           ORDER BY SUM(cost) DESC
         ) AS rn
  FROM azure_consumption
  WHERE billing_date >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '30 days'
  GROUP BY cost_centre, resource_id
) t
WHERE rn <= 3
ORDER BY cost_centre, total_cost DESC;
8. Difference between window functions and GROUP BY?
GROUP BY collapses rows into aggregates; window functions compute aggregates alongside each row without collapsing. Use windows for ranking, running totals, per-partition top-N — the query above is a classic example.
9. Regularisation — L1 vs L2 vs ElasticNet?
L1 (Lasso) drives coefficients to zero — feature selection. L2 (Ridge) shrinks smoothly — handles multicollinearity. ElasticNet mixes both — useful when you want selection but have correlated groups of features.
10. Feature engineering — walk me through one example.
In credit risk: raw features were income, EMI, tenure. Engineered: DTI = EMI/income, payment-to-income ratio, count of late payments in last 6/12/24 months, ratio of revolving to installment credit. These lifted AUC by ~4 points because they encoded behaviour, not just state.

7 Behavioural Q&A drills (STAR)

Tell me about a time you influenced a business decision.
S: Cost-centre owners at Manulife were disputing anomaly alerts. T: Get buy-in without watering down detection. A: Built a Grafana view showing each alert with the Prophet forecast band and the offending resources; ran a workshop with FinOps to co-design the alert thresholds. R: Adoption jumped, complaints dropped, and the platform became the FinOps source of truth — enabling the $12.8M savings.
A time you disagreed with a stakeholder.
Risk team wanted a pure logistic regression for PD "because it's explainable". I proposed a champion-challenger: logistic in production, XGBoost + SHAP as challenger. Once SHAP outputs proved interpretable and the AUC lift was material, we promoted XGBoost. Lesson: don't argue explainability in the abstract — show it.
A failure and what you learned.
Early Prophet rollout: alerts were too noisy on small cost-centres because I only used percentage CIs. Missed adoption in week 1. Fix: added absolute-dollar floor and consecutive-day rule. Learning: ship the alerting logic with the same rigour as the model.
Working with ambiguity.
The GenAI fraud assistant started with "help investigators go faster" — no metric. I ran three investigator interviews, defined "time to triage" as the target, agreed a baseline, and only then chose the RAG architecture. The metric drove the design.
Leading without authority.
Ran a weekly ML office hour for the Manulife analytics team — reviewed each other's models, shared feature-engineering patterns. Not a formal role, but standardised how the team validated and documented models.

8 GenAI / LLM Q&A drills

Explain RAG in one minute.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation grounds an LLM in your own data: (1) chunk documents, (2) embed chunks into a vector store (FAISS in my case), (3) at query time embed the question, retrieve top-k chunks, (4) stuff them into the LLM prompt with the question. It reduces hallucination and enables citations.
Chunking strategy — what did you use?
Started with recursive character splitting (~500–800 tokens, 10–15% overlap). For structured docs (tables, claims forms), switched to semantic/section-aware chunking so a claim row wasn't split across chunks. Always store metadata (source, page, chunk_id) so citations are possible.
Why FAISS over pgvector or a managed vector DB?
FAISS was ideal for local/experimental workloads — fast, no infra. For enterprise Manulife-style deployment I'd choose pgvector (already have Postgres) or Azure AI Search for hybrid keyword+vector, RBAC, and managed scaling.
How do you evaluate a RAG system?
Two layers: retrieval (recall@k, MRR on a labelled question–chunk set) and generation (faithfulness — does the answer stick to retrieved context; answer relevance; and human eval for high-stakes cases). Frameworks: RAGAS, TruLens, or a bespoke LLM-as-judge with guardrails.
Hallucination — how do you reduce it?
Ground with RAG, force citations in the prompt, lower temperature, use "answer only from context; if not present say I don't know", and validate output with a second LLM or rules pass. For fraud, we also required structured JSON output validated against a schema.
Prompt injection — how do you defend?
Separate system and user prompts, strip/escape retrieved content, use allow-lists for tool calls, and for NL-to-SQL enforce a read-only DB role and a SQL parser that rejects DDL/DML. Never trust the retrieved chunk to be benign.
Agent vs chain — when do you use which?
Chain = deterministic sequence; use when steps are known. Agent = LLM decides which tool to call next; use when the path is data-dependent (e.g. NL-to-SQL where the model must inspect schema, draft, validate, retry).

9 Questions to ask the interviewer

Ask 2 sharp questions. Prefer ones that signal seniority and delivery orientation.

  1. "What does the ML lifecycle look like here — do data scientists own deployment and monitoring, or is there a separate MLOps function?"
  2. "Where is the team on the classical-ML vs GenAI curve today, and where do you want it in 12 months?"
  3. "What's the biggest model or product decision the team is wrestling with right now?"
  4. "How is model impact measured — is it tied to business KPIs or ML metrics?"
  5. "If I joined, what would a successful first 90 days look like?"
