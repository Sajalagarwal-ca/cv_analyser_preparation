How They Sell (Distribution Model)

1. Intermediated Retail (the core engine)

AGF does not sell funds directly to the public. It sells through independent financial advisors and dealers (IIROC/CIRO dealers, mutual fund dealers and bank channels). Wholesalers (external and internal sales reps) cover advisors. In Q3 AGF paid $40.7M in trailing commissions in a single quarter, which shows how advisor-centric the model is.

2. ETFs & SMAs / Model Delivery

This is the fastest-growing channel. ETF and SMA AUM rose from $3.5B to $5.5B year over year. AGF supplies model portfolios to third-party managed-account platforms and earns a fee without custody of the assets. Its U.S. Large Cap Growth strategy was recently added to a leading U.S. advisory platform.

3. Institutional

Segregated mandates and sub-advisory work for pensions, sovereign wealth funds, endowments and foundations. These sales run on RFPs, due-diligence questionnaires and consultant relationships, all of which are heavy on documents and well suited to GenAI.

4. Direct HNW (Private Wealth)

Relationship managers at the boutique wealth firms work directly with clients. The work centers on client reviews, financial planning and reporting.

Key insight for the interview: The AI CoE's first priorities are Investment Management (PMs, analysts, research) and Distribution (wholesalers, advisors, marketing, RFPs). These are the two revenue engines: investment performance drives flows, and distribution converts performance into sales. Frame every idea you raise as either "better or faster investment decisions" or "more productive sales conversations".

5. Role Decode: Forward Deployed AI Engineer

What it really is: An internal FDE. Your "customers" are AGF's own portfolio managers, analysts, wholesalers, marketing and operations teams. You will be a founding member of the FDE team inside the AI Centre of Expertise, working as an individual contributor (IC) who owns the full cycle: discovery, build, deploy, adoption and support.

4 Responsibility Pillars

  1. Business engagement: discovery workshops, process analysis, scoring ideas on value, feasibility and risk, acting as a trusted advisor
  2. AI solution engineering: agents, Copilot solutions, RAG, intelligent workflows, integrations and reusable frameworks
  3. Product delivery & ops: production deployment, monitoring, adoption and enhancement
  4. Governance, risk & compliance: architecture reviews, risk assessments and responsible AI, working with Legal, Privacy, Cyber and Audit

Tech Stack Signals

Required: Advanced Python, AI agents, prompt engineering & orchestration, RAG, APIs & integration, GitHub/DevOps, enterprise data integration, SDLC

Preferred: Azure AI Foundry, Azure OpenAI, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Power Platform, Azure Cloud, Databricks, vector DBs, knowledge graphs, ML/analytics, investment/distribution workflows

→ They are a Microsoft shop. Expect M365 Copilot rollout plus custom agents in Foundry and Copilot Studio.

Success Measures (how you'll be judged; mirror these words)

  • Business value delivered and contribution to revenue-generating initiatives
  • User adoption, engagement and stakeholder satisfaction
  • Number and quality of production AI solutions shipped
  • Reliability and maintainability, plus reuse of components and patterns
  • Governance and compliance alignment, and growth of AGF's AI maturity

9. Likely Questions & Answer Outlines

Q1. Why AGF, and why this role?
Founding-team opportunity; a mid-size firm where AI can measurably move the business; Investment and Distribution are both areas where you have finance domain depth; you are already an Azure and Microsoft-stack engineer; and you want to move from vendor to owner.
Q2. What does "forward deployed" mean to you?
"Engineering embedded with the business. You own the outcome, not the ticket. You discover the real problem, ship a small working version fast, measure adoption, then harden and reuse it. The hard part is rarely the model; it's workflow fit, data access and trust."
Q3. Tell me about a time you identified a problem the business hadn't articulated.
Cloud cost story: nobody owned overspend. Use the STAR format: S (unowned Azure spend across cost centers), T (find and stop waste), A (Prophet forecast, resource-level anomaly detection, automated owner alerts, Grafana and Power BI), R ($12.8M CAD per year, a governance process adopted by owners).
Q4. Walk me through a GenAI/RAG solution you built. How did you handle hallucinations?
RAG Document Assistant or Fraud Copilot. Cover chunking, embeddings, FAISS retrieval and citations (file, page, chunk ID). For grounding: answer only from retrieved context, "I don't know" fallback, and an eval set. Mention what you'd add in production: hybrid search, reranking, an eval harness (faithfulness, recall@k), and access controls on documents.
Q5. How would you ensure AI solutions are compliant in a regulated asset manager?
Data classification and entitlements (PMs see only what they're allowed to); Azure OpenAI in AGF's tenant with no training on data; PII redaction; logging and audit trail of prompts and responses; human-in-the-loop for anything client-facing (sales communications must go through compliance review); model risk documentation; involve Legal, Compliance and Cyber early, not at the end.
Q6. How do you work with a non-technical stakeholder, e.g. a portfolio manager?
Shadow their workflow for an hour. Quantify time spent. Build a clickable prototype in days (Streamlit or Copilot Studio). Let them break it. Iterate weekly. Report in their language (hours saved, decisions faster), not model metrics.
Q7. Your experience with Copilot Studio / AI Foundry / Power Platform?
Be honest and show momentum: "My production work has been code-first on Azure: Azure OpenAI, Functions, ADF, Databricks. I've been getting hands-on with AI Foundry agents and Copilot Studio [only say this if you have done the lab]. Those are the low-code front ends for the same patterns I've built in Python. I'd use Copilot Studio for fast M365-native experiences and Foundry or code for complex, integrated agents."
Q8. How do you decide what to build first?
Value × feasibility × risk scoring. Start with an internal, human-in-the-loop, read-only quick win with a clear owner and measurable baseline. Prove adoption, then expand.
Q9. Your titles say "Data Scientist". Are you a software engineer?
"I build and ship applications, not just notebooks: FastAPI services, Streamlit apps, Azure Functions, ADF pipelines, deployed and monitored in production. The $12.8M system is an end-to-end application with scheduled jobs, alerting and dashboards."
