GLP-1 (Semaglutide 0.25 mg) — Indian Vegetarian Diet & Lifestyle Guide

Personalized starter guide · Starting weight ~120 kg · Dose week 1: 0.25 mg (initiation dose) · Vegetarian, Indian cuisine

Important medical disclaimer: This is educational content, not medical advice. Always follow the prescribing doctor's instructions. Contact the doctor immediately for severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, signs of dehydration, jaundice, or allergic reactions. GLP-1 medications are not safe during pregnancy — use reliable birth control while on therapy.

1. How GLP-1 Works & What Week 1 Feels Like

Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy) mimics the natural GLP-1 hormone. It works in four ways: triggers insulin release, blocks glucagon, slows stomach emptying, and increases satiety by acting on the brain's hunger centers (Cleveland Clinic).

Most common side effects (especially first weeks & dose increases): nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, loss of appetite, indigestion, mild headache, fatigue, injection site itchiness. These usually improve within 1–2 weeks.
Call the doctor immediately if: severe upper-abdominal pain radiating to back (pancreatitis), persistent vomiting, dehydration, gallbladder pain, yellowing skin/eyes, lump in the neck or trouble swallowing, vision changes, or signs of low blood sugar (shakiness, sweating, confusion) — especially if she also takes insulin or sulfonylureas.

4. Foods to Emphasize vs. Limit

✅ Emphasize

  • Dals: moong, masoor, toor, chana
  • Paneer (grilled), tofu, curd, buttermilk
  • Sabzi: lauki, tinda, palak, methi, bhindi, baingan, cabbage, cauliflower, capsicum
  • Whole grains: bajra, jowar, oats, ragi, quinoa, brown rice (small portion)
  • Fruit: papaya, guava, apple, pear, berries, orange (low-GI)
  • Ginger, jeera, saunf, ajwain, hing — help with nausea & bloating
  • Nuts/seeds: almonds, walnuts, flax, chia, pumpkin (portion controlled)
  • Cooking fats: mustard oil, cold-pressed groundnut, ghee (1 tsp/meal max)

⛔ Limit / Avoid

  • Deep-fried: samosa, pakora, poori, bhatura, vada
  • Refined carbs: maida, white bread, biscuits, naan, white rice in bulk
  • Sweets: mithai, gulab jamun, jalebi, halwa, sugary chai
  • Sugary/fizzy drinks, packaged juices
  • Very oily curries (paneer butter masala, malai kofta)
  • Large, heavy meals — GLP-1 slows digestion → nausea
  • Alcohol (worsens nausea, hypoglycemia risk)
  • High-fat dairy in excess (full-cream milk, malai, cheese loads)
  • Very spicy / chili-heavy food in first 2 weeks

6. Managing Side Effects — Practical Playbook

Nausea

  • Smaller portions, eat slowly, stop at first fullness
  • Ginger tea, saunf water, or 1 tsp jeera-hing water
  • Avoid greasy/fried and very spicy food in week 1–2
  • Bland options: khichdi, curd rice, moong dal soup, dalia
  • Don't lie down for 60 min after eating

Constipation

  • 2.5–3 L water/day (sip constantly)
  • Soaked flax/chia (1 tsp AM + 1 tsp PM)
  • Isabgol (psyllium) 1 tsp at bedtime with warm water
  • Papaya, prunes, pear
  • 10-minute walk after each meal

Diarrhea

  • ORS or nimbu-pani with salt
  • BRAT-style Indian: banana, curd rice, khichdi, applesauce, toast
  • Skip milk temporarily (lactose sensitivity)

Reflux / burping / bloating

  • 4–5 small meals, not 3 large
  • Avoid onion, garlic, tomato heavy dishes if triggering
  • Ajwain water after meals

Fatigue / weakness

  • Check she's actually eating enough — not skipping meals
  • B12 + Vitamin D + iron levels (common veg deficiencies)
  • Electrolytes if sweating / diarrhea

7. Hydration, Supplements & Micronutrients

  • Water: 2.5–3 L/day. Use a marked bottle. Add mint, lemon, cucumber.
  • Protein powder (optional): 1 scoop whey isolate or plant blend if she can't hit 90 g via food.
  • Multivitamin: Once daily (covers gaps as intake drops).
  • Vitamin B12: Vegetarians commonly deficient — get levels checked.
  • Vitamin D3: 1000–2000 IU/day (confirm with doctor).
  • Calcium: 1000 mg via curd, milk, tofu, ragi, til.
  • Omega-3: Flax, chia, walnuts; algae-based DHA supplement if needed.
  • Iron: Palak, methi, jaggery, sprouts + vitamin C food (nimbu, amla) for absorption.
  • Electrolytes: Coconut water 2–3× / week, especially if nauseated.

9. Injection & Storage Tips (Semaglutide Pen)

  • Same day each week; time of day doesn't matter, with or without food.
  • Rotate injection sites: belly (2 inches from navel), outer thigh, back of upper arm.
  • Unopened pens: refrigerate (2–8 °C). In-use pen: many pens allow room temperature (max 30 °C) for a set number of days — check the specific pen leaflet.
  • Never freeze. Never share pens or needles.
  • If a dose is missed by ≤5 days, take it as soon as remembered. If >5 days, skip and resume schedule. Confirm with the prescribing doctor.
  • Dispose needles in a puncture-proof container.

11. Realistic Expectations

  • Weeks 1–4 (0.25 mg): This is a tolerance dose, not a fat-loss dose. She may only lose 1–3 kg. Appetite suppression can be mild.
  • Weeks 5–8 (0.5 mg, if doctor titrates): Appetite drops noticeably. Portion control becomes automatic.
  • Months 3–6: Typical semaglutide loss is ~10–15% body weight over 12 months when combined with diet + activity. At 120 kg baseline that's ~12–18 kg.
  • Plateaus are normal — do not cut calories further. Increase protein, strength train, sleep 7–8 hrs.
